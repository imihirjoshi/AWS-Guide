Never used AWS before? Start here.

Ten minutes. No jargon. By the end you will know what the cloud actually is and which eight services cover most of what people build.

1. What is the cloud, really

Amazon owns very large buildings full of computers. You rent a slice of them by the hour. That is the whole idea.

Before the cloud, you bought a server, put it in your office, and it sat idle most of the time. If it broke, you fixed it. If your site got popular on a Monday, you could not buy a second server by Tuesday.

Now you rent, and you give it back when you are done.

The one thing that changes everything: you pay for what you use, by the hour or by the request. So a mistake you forget about keeps charging you until you notice. Set a budget alert on day one.

2. Why there are 300 services

It looks mad. It is actually simple. Every service answers one question, and AWS just never deletes anything.

The questionThe answer
Where does my code run?EC2, Lambda, Fargate, App Runner
Where do my files live?S3, EBS, EFS
Where does my data live?RDS, Aurora, DynamoDB
How do users reach me?Route 53, CloudFront, Load Balancer
Who is allowed in?IAM, Cognito
Is it working?CloudWatch, CloudTrail

Ask which question you have. Then only three or four services matter.

3. The eight to learn first

Learn these and you can build most things. Everything else can wait until you actually need it.

4. How the money works

Four things are charged, and only four things surprise people.

  • Time. A server that is on costs money, even at 3am with nobody using it.
  • Storage. Per GB per month. A disk you forgot to delete still bills.
  • Requests. Per million calls. Usually small.
  • Data going out. Data in is usually free. Data out to the internet is not. This is the one that catches everybody.
The three classic bill shocks.
A NAT gateway left running, roughly 33 dollars a month doing nothing.
CloudWatch logs with no retention set, growing forever.
A test server started on a Friday and forgotten until the bill arrives.

5. The mistakes to avoid on day one

  1. Turn on MFA on the root account. Then stop using the root account.
  2. Set a budget alert. Five minutes. It has saved more people more money than any other single action.
  3. Never put a key in your code. Anything pushed to GitHub is found by bots in minutes, not days.
  4. Do not make an S3 bucket public unless you mean it. Most leaked-data news stories are exactly this.
  5. Pick one region and stay in it. Mumbai is ap-south-1 and it is closest for Indian users. Resources in another region are invisible in the console until you switch.
  6. Tag everything. Later, when the bill is confusing, tags are the only way to know what belongs to what.

6. Now go and play

The playground lets you drag services onto a board, connect them, change the sizes and see roughly what it would cost. Start with the "Classic web app" template and change one thing at a time.

Then browse all the services. The ones marked core are the ones worth reading first.
