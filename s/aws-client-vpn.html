AWS Client VPN

Networking and Content Delivery  ·  Common, learn when you need it

Lets staff on laptops anywhere dial into your private AWS network.

Picture it as

A staff side door with a key card, for people working from home.

What it actually does

You set up an endpoint, users install an OpenVPN style client, and once connected their laptop can reach private servers as if it were in the office. It handles logins through certificates, Active Directory or a federated identity provider. It is per user remote access, and it is not cheap once you have many people online.

Reach for it when

  • Developers need to reach a private database that has no public address
  • Staff work from home and need internal tools
  • A contractor needs limited access for a short project

Do not reach for it when

  • You are joining two networks, an office to AWS, not individual people. Use aws-site-to-site-vpn.
  • You just want to reach one EC2 box for admin. Session Manager needs no VPN at all.
  • You want per app access without a network tunnel. A zero trust proxy fits better than this.

One small example

Twenty engineers connect each morning and can reach the private RDS instance from their laptops.

How you pay

About 10 cents per hour for the endpoint in each subnet, plus about 5 cents per connected user per hour. The surprise is the subnet charge ticks along all night even when nobody is connected.
