Amazon Verified Permissions

Security and Identity  ·  Common, learn when you need it

Decides whether a user is allowed to do a thing in your app, so you do not code it yourself.

Picture it as

A doorman with the guest list, instead of every room checking names itself.

What it actually does

You write the rules in a language called Cedar, store them here, and your app asks a yes or no question for each action. It keeps permission rules out of your application code, so they can be changed and reviewed on their own. It is for permissions inside your own application, not for who can touch AWS resources.

Reach for it when

  • A SaaS app where each customer sets their own sharing rules
  • Rules keep changing and you are tired of editing if statements
  • Auditors want to see the permission rules written down in one place

Do not reach for it when

  • You want to control access to AWS itself, like who may stop an EC2 instance. That is aws-identity-and-access-management.
  • Your app has three simple roles. A table in your own database is simpler and free.
  • You cannot afford an extra network call in a hot path. Cache decisions or check locally instead.

One small example

A document app asks whether Priya may share a folder, and the answer comes back from a Cedar rule, not app code.

How you pay

Per authorization call, fractions of a cent each. The surprise is volume. If you check permissions on every item in a list, calls multiply fast, so use the batch call.
