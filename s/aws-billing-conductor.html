AWS Billing Conductor

Cost and Billing  ·  Advanced, most people never need it

It lets you send someone a different AWS bill than the one AWS actually produced.

Picture it as

A landlord who pays one electricity bill for the whole building, then works out what each flat owes, with his own rates and a service charge added.

What it actually does

You group accounts into billing groups and set your own prices, discounts or flat fees. It produces a second, made-up bill for that group, while AWS still charges you the real amount. It is for resellers, managed service providers and big companies that charge internal departments. Almost nobody else needs it.

Reach for it when

  • You resell AWS to customers and need to bill them your own rates
  • You charge internal business units for their cloud use
  • You must hide AWS discounts from the people you invoice

Do not reach for it when

  • You only want to see which team spent what, use cost allocation tags with Cost Explorer
  • You just want a spending limit, use AWS Budgets

One small example

An IT company hosts 12 clients in separate AWS accounts and invoices each one at list price plus 15 percent, generated automatically each month.

How you pay

A fee per account per month in each billing group. The surprise is that you still pay AWS the real bill in full, so this is pure extra cost on top.
