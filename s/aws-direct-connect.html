AWS Direct Connect

Networking and Content Delivery  ·  Common, learn when you need it

A private cable from your building or data centre into AWS, instead of going over the internet.

Picture it as

A private road from your warehouse to the motorway, so your lorries never sit in public traffic.

What it actually does

A dedicated fibre link between your network and AWS, ordered through a partner at a location where AWS has equipment. It gives steadier speed and lower delay than the internet, and it lowers the price of data leaving AWS. It is not encrypted by itself, and setup takes weeks or months, not minutes.

Reach for it when

  • Moving large amounts of data in and out every day
  • Applications that break when latency jitters
  • Rules that say your traffic must not cross the public internet

Do not reach for it when

  • You need something working today, use AWS Site-to-Site VPN
  • Small or occasional traffic, the internet is cheaper and fine
  • Only want privacy, not speed, a VPN gives you that at far less cost

One small example

A media firm pushes a terabyte of daily footage to S3 over a 10 Gbps Direct Connect link instead of saturating its office line.

How you pay

An hourly port charge whether you use it or not, plus per GB out at a reduced rate. The surprise is the separate bill from the telecom partner for the last stretch of fibre to your building, which AWS does not include.
