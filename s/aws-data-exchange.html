AWS Data Exchange

Analytics  ·  Advanced, most people never need it

A shop inside AWS where you buy or sell data sets and keep them updated automatically.

Picture it as

A magazine subscription. You pay, and new issues arrive on your doorstep without you asking each time.

What it actually does

Sellers list data products, weather history, stock prices, health statistics, and buyers subscribe. The data lands in your own S3 bucket, or is queryable through Redshift or an API, and refreshes when the seller publishes an update. A lot of the free listings are public data you could fetch yourself. It only makes sense when you want billing, licensing and delivery handled in one place.

Reach for it when

  • Buying a commercial data feed and wanting it delivered into your account
  • Selling data to other AWS customers with billing handled for you
  • Replacing a manual quarterly file download from a vendor

Do not reach for it when

  • For free public data, check the AWS Open Data Registry or the source directly, it costs nothing
  • For sharing data with one named partner, use S3 cross-account access or Lake Formation sharing
  • For a joint analysis where neither side may see the other's rows, use AWS Clean Rooms

One small example

A lender subscribes to a weekly postcode-level property price file and it appears in S3 every Monday.

How you pay

The subscription price the seller sets, often thousands per year, plus normal S3 storage and transfer. The catch is that subscriptions can auto-renew.

Amazon Simple Storage ServiceA place to keep files, as many as you want.Amazon RedshiftA warehouse built for asking big questions of big data.AWS Lake FormationDecides who is allowed to see which tables, columns and rows of your data in S3.AWS GlueCleans and moves data between places.
