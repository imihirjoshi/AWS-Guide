AWS Entity Resolution

Analytics  ·  Advanced, most people never need it

Works out which records in different files are the same person or company.

Picture it as

Merging two address books where one says Rob Smith, 12 High St and the other says Robert Smith, 12 High Street. A careful assistant marks them as one person.

What it actually does

You point it at data in S3 and it matches records using rules you write, a built-in machine learning matcher, or a third party service. It hands back groups of records that look like the same entity, with a confidence level. It does not clean your data or store a master record for you, you still decide what to do with the matches. This is a narrow tool, most teams solve this with SQL or a library until the data gets genuinely large and messy.

Reach for it when

  • Joining customer records across systems with no shared ID
  • De-duplicating a marketing list before a campaign
  • Building a single customer view from several sources

Do not reach for it when

  • If your records share a reliable ID, a plain SQL join is cheaper and exact
  • For fixing formats, blank fields and typos, use AWS Glue DataBrew or Glue first
  • For matching across two companies who may not share data, use AWS Clean Rooms

One small example

A hotel group finds that one guest exists three times because of two email addresses and a misspelt surname.

How you pay

Per record processed each time you run a matching job. Re-running a large list often, or matching against a paid third party provider, is where the bill grows.
