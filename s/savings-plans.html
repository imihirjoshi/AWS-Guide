Savings Plans

Cost and Billing  ·  Common, learn when you need it

A pricing commitment, not a service. You promise to spend a set amount per hour for one or three years and pay less per hour.

Picture it as

A yearly train pass instead of buying a ticket each day. Cheaper per trip, but you pay for the pass whether you travel or not.

What it actually does

Savings Plans is a way of paying, not something you switch on and use. You commit to spend a fixed amount of money per hour, for one year or three years, and in return AWS charges you lower rates, up to about 72 percent below on demand. The discount is applied to your bill automatically each hour, across your accounts in AWS Organizations. There are different plans. Compute Savings Plans are the most flexible and cover Amazon EC2, AWS Lambda and AWS Fargate across instance families, sizes, operating systems and Regions. EC2 Instance Savings Plans give a bigger discount but lock you to one instance family in one Region. There are also plans for Amazon SageMaker AI. You can pay all up front, part up front, or nothing up front, with a better rate the more you pay early. Once bought, a plan cannot be cancelled or refunded.

Reach for it when

  • You have steady baseline usage that runs all year and is not going away
  • You want a discount without picking exact instance types, so a Compute Savings Plan fits
  • You already know your lowest monthly compute spend and can commit to a part of it safely
  • You run Lambda or Fargate a lot, since Reserved Instances do not cover those but Compute Savings Plans do

Do not reach for it when

  • Not for short lived or spiky workloads. You pay the commitment even on quiet hours.
  • Not for a new workload you cannot size yet. Run on demand first, look at AWS Cost Explorer, then commit.
  • Not a fault tolerance or batch discount. For interruptible work, Spot Instances are cheaper.
  • Do not commit to 100 percent of your usage. Cover the floor, not the peak.
  • Do not buy one expecting to change your mind. There is no cancel and no refund.

One small example

A team always runs at least 20 servers day and night. They look at Cost Explorer, commit to the spend those 20 cover for three years, and their bill for that part drops sharply. The extra servers they add in busy weeks stay on demand.

How you pay

There is no fee for a Savings Plan itself. You pay the hourly amount you committed to, every hour of the term, used or not. Usage above the commitment is billed at normal on demand rates. Paying more up front lowers the rate.
