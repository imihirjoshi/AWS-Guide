Amazon WorkSpaces

End User Computing  ·  Common, learn when you need it

Gives each person a Windows or Linux desktop they open from any device.

Picture it as

A locker at work. Your things stay in it, and you can open it from any branch.

What it actually does

AWS runs the desktop in the cloud and streams the screen to a client app or browser. The desktop keeps your files and settings between sessions. Performance depends heavily on the user's internet, and Windows licensing rules can be awkward if you bring your own.

Reach for it when

  • Contractors need access without company laptops
  • Staff must work from home on a locked down machine
  • Data must never sit on a personal device

Do not reach for it when

  • If you only need one application, not a whole desktop, AppStream 2.0 is cheaper.
  • If people only need a safe browser, use WorkSpaces Secure Browser.
  • For developers who just need a Linux shell, an amazon-ec2 instance is simpler and cheaper.
  • With bad or high latency internet, this feels terrible. Do not force it.

One small example

A support team of 200 contractors in another country logs into WorkSpaces each morning, and no customer data leaves AWS.

How you pay

Monthly per desktop, or hourly plus a smaller monthly fee. The surprise is the monthly option charges a full month even if someone logs in once, and idle desktops keep billing until you delete them.
