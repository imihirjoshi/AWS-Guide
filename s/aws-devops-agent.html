AWS DevOps Agent

Management and Governance  ·  Common, learn when you need it

An AI assistant that digs into why your system broke and suggests the fix.

Picture it as

An on-call colleague who reads all the logs before you wake up.

What it actually does

It connects to your logs, metrics, code repositories and deployment pipelines, then works out what changed and what is failing. It went generally available in March 2026 and also looks at Azure and on-premises systems. It is new, so treat its conclusions as a strong first draft, not a verdict, and it needs real access to your telemetry and code before it is any use.

Reach for it when

  • An incident is live and nobody knows which deploy caused it
  • You want preventive advice from past incidents rather than a fresh guess each time
  • A small team has no dedicated SRE

Do not reach for it when

  • If you have no logs or metrics wired up yet, fix that first with Amazon CloudWatch. The agent has nothing to read otherwise.
  • If you want simple threshold alerts, a CloudWatch alarm is cheaper and more predictable.
  • If your rules require a human to approve every production change, keep it as an advisor and do not let it act.

One small example

Latency jumps at 2am, and by morning it has pointed at a config change in yesterday's deploy and named the pull request.

How you pay

Charged for use, with monthly credits included depending on your AWS Support plan. The surprise is that heavy investigation of large log volumes also raises your CloudWatch and data transfer bills.
