AWS PrivateLink

Networking and Content Delivery  ·  Common, learn when you need it

Lets your private network reach an AWS service or a partner's service without touching the internet.

Picture it as

A service hatch built straight through the wall into the shop next door, so deliveries never go out onto the street.

What it actually does

It puts a private address inside your own VPC that points at a service somewhere else, an AWS service like S3, or software sold by another company, or another team's application. Traffic stays on the AWS network. The link is one way, the consumer calls the provider, and the provider cannot call back.

Reach for it when

  • Servers with no internet access that still need to call AWS APIs
  • Buying SaaS that must not be reached over the internet
  • Exposing one internal API to another AWS account without peering

Do not reach for it when

  • Two VPCs that need full two way access, use peering or AWS Transit Gateway
  • Reaching your own office network, use Site-to-Site VPN or Direct Connect

One small example

A database server in a private subnet writes to S3 through a VPC endpoint, with no NAT gateway and no route to the internet.

How you pay

An hourly charge per endpoint per availability zone, plus per GB. Teams create endpoints for a dozen services across three zones and are surprised by the hourly fees, which add up before any data moves.
