AWS Verified Access

Networking and Content Delivery  ·  Advanced, most people never need it

Staff reach internal apps over the internet without a VPN, and every request is checked.

Picture it as

A doorman who checks your ID and that your coat is buttoned, every single time you walk in, instead of one lock on the front door.

What it actually does

Verified Access puts a policy in front of an internal application. Each request is evaluated against who the user is, from IAM Identity Center or an OIDC provider, and optionally the device's health from a tool like CrowdStrike or Jamf. It is AWS's take on zero trust access. It only covers applications you host inside AWS, so it does not replace a VPN that also reaches on-premise networks or non-HTTP services.

Reach for it when

  • Replacing a VPN for internal web apps hosted in AWS
  • You want device posture checked on every request
  • Per-application access rules instead of full network access

Do not reach for it when

  • Not for public, customer-facing apps, use Cognito or your own login plus WAF
  • If staff need SSH, RDP or on-premise networks, keep a VPN or use Client VPN
  • Small teams with one internal tool will find Cognito plus a load balancer simpler

One small example

A contractor opens the internal dashboard in a browser, and access is denied because their laptop disk is not encrypted.

How you pay

Per application per hour, plus per gigabyte of data processed. The hourly charge runs per application whether anyone uses it or not.
