Who wrote this

Engineer and founder  ·  Ahmedabad, India

Every explanation on this site was written by Mihir Joshi, an engineer and founder in India who builds and runs production software for education and pharmaceutical companies.

Why I wrote this

Most AWS documentation is written for people who already understand AWS. It explains a service using words that only make sense if you already know what those words mean. That leaves a lot of capable people stuck at the first step, quietly assuming everyone else already knows.

This is the version I wanted when I started. Every service gets one plain sentence, an everyday comparison, and the thing nobody publishes: when it is the wrong choice, and what to use instead.

Why that matters for this site

I am not an AWS trainer. I am someone who runs real systems and pays the real bills. The cost figures, the capacity ceilings and the "what breaks first" notes come from operating production, not from a course. When a page says a NAT gateway will quietly cost you about thirty three dollars a month doing nothing, that is because somebody has paid it.

It also means I have limits, and the site states them. Where something could not be confirmed against a primary AWS source, the page says so rather than guessing. There is a list of unconfirmed items on the certifications page for exactly that reason.

How the writing was done

The explanations are original. They were researched against the official AWS documentation, service quota pages, pricing pages and public engineering writeups, then written in plain English and fact checked. Where a service is retired, renamed, or something almost nobody needs, the page says that instead of describing it as current.

AI tooling was used in the research and drafting, as it is in most technical writing now. Every claim was checked against a primary source before it was published, and anything that could not be verified is marked as unverified rather than quietly dropped. The judgement, the structure and the final word are mine.
